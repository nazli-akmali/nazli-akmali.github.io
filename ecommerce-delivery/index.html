E-commerce Delivery Performance

When do orders arrive late, and what drives it?

An interactive dashboard built on the public Olist Brazilian e-commerce dataset (January 2017 to August 2018). It tracks late deliveries over time, links them to shipping delays and ranks sellers by how often they dispatch late.

Key findings

Late deliveries cluster in time and start before the parcel leaves the seller

Overall, 6.79% of delivered orders arrived after the estimated date, with an average delivery time of 12.54 days. That average hides three patterns.

18.96%

Late-delivery rate in March 2018, the peak month and nearly 2.8× the overall rate. November 2017 was a second spike at around 12%.

7.5×

Orders handed to the carrier more than 3 days late were late 41.85% of the time, against 5.57% for orders shipped on time.

49–90%

Share of late dispatches among the ten worst sellers with at least 30 orders. For the worst one, 9 in 10 shipments left late.

Method

  • Headline KPIs and the monthly trend use all delivered orders
  • Shipping-delay and seller analyses use single-item delivered orders only, so each delay can be attributed to one seller
  • Seller ranking is limited to sellers with at least 30 eligible orders

Questions I'd explore next

  • What caused the March 2018 peak: order volume, carriers or regions?
  • Would dispatch deadlines or alerts for repeat-late sellers reduce late deliveries?
  • How do late deliveries affect review scores and repeat purchases?
