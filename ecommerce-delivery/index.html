E-commerce Delivery Performance

When do deliveries run late, and how are they linked to shipping delays and seller performance?

A self-directed analysis of historical orders from the public Olist Brazilian e-commerce dataset. It looks at delivery reliability over time, whether sellers hand orders to the carrier before their shipping deadline, and how late-shipment rates differ between sellers.

Analytical approach

From raw tables to a dashboard

  1. 01

    Data exploration & preparation

    Imported order and order-item tables, then checked order statuses, missing timestamps and how the tables join, so items aren't counted as orders.

  2. 02

    KPI definition & SQL analysis

    Defined late-delivery rate, actual delivery time, shipping-deadline compliance and seller late-shipment rate, using CTEs, conditional aggregation and window functions.

  3. 03

    Segmentation & investigation

    Compared monthly trends, order structures, shipping-delay severity and sellers, with a minimum-volume rule for seller comparisons.

  4. 04

    Dashboard development & validation

    Built PostgreSQL views for reporting, designed the Tableau dashboard on top of them and cross-checked its figures against SQL queries.

Key findings

Late deliveries cluster in time and are strongly associated with dispatch delays

Overall, 6.79% of delivered orders arrived after the estimated date, and delivery took 12.54 days on average. Behind that average are three patterns.

18.96%

Peak monthly late-delivery rate

March 2018, about 2.8× the overall rate of 6.79%. November 2017 was also elevated, at around 12%.

Spikes this large point to specific periods worth investigating, not a constant background rate.

7.5×

Higher late-delivery rate when dispatch is 3+ days late

Single-item orders handed to the carrier more than 3 days after the shipping deadline: 41.85% late, against 5.57% for orders handed over on time.

Shipping-deadline compliance is visible days before delivery, which makes it a candidate early-warning signal.

~49–90%

Late-shipment rates of the ten highest-ranked sellers

Each seller's share of orders handed over after the deadline, among sellers with at least 30 eligible orders.

A small group of sellers misses deadlines on most orders, making them a focused starting point for follow-up.

This is a descriptive analysis of historical data. The patterns are associations and do not establish causes.

Methodology

What each number measures

Late delivery
Customer delivery after the estimated delivery date, compared by calendar day.
Late shipment
Carrier handover after the seller's shipping deadline, compared by exact timestamp.
Seller threshold
Only sellers with at least 30 eligible orders are ranked, to avoid over-reading extreme rates from a handful of orders. It reduces, but does not remove, statistical uncertainty.

Populations

  • Headline KPIs and the monthly trend use delivered orders with valid delivery and estimate dates.
  • Shipping-delay and seller analyses use delivered single-item orders with the needed shipping timestamps, so each handover links to exactly one seller and one deadline.
  • Comparisons are not adjusted for region, carrier, product category or season.

Business implications

Where an operations team could look next

These are recommendations and open questions. None of them was implemented or tested in this project.

Recommended investigations

  • Break down the March 2018 spike by region, carrier, product mix and order volume
  • Monitor shipping-deadline compliance, especially handovers more than three days late
  • Prioritise follow-up with eligible sellers whose late-shipment rates stay high, considering order volume alongside the rate

Open analytical questions

  • Could alerts for orders past their shipping limit flag at-risk deliveries early enough to act?
  • How do late deliveries relate to review scores and repeat purchases?
  • Do the seller differences hold within the same region and product category?
