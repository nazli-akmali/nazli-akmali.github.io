This spec is a review and rewrite of the feature idea, based on how the current MVP works. Every change from the original idea has a reason, so you can push back on any of them.

1. Review: what to change and why

# Original idea Problem Change
1 Target estimates use "activity level" and also "distinguish everyday activity from exercise" Standard activity multipliers (×1.2…×1.9) already include exercise. Using one of them and also adding exercise counts the same activity twice. Use a baseline multiplier for everyday activity only. Add exercise separately from logged sessions (MET × weight × hours), averaged per day. See §3.2.
2 "The sex parameter required by the selected calculation method" Some users won't want to answer, or won't fit either option. Ask for it as "sex used by the formula" and explain why it's needed. Offer "prefer not to say", which shows the estimate as a range covering both formula constants (about 166 kcal apart) instead of blocking the estimate.
3 Estimates for everyone Adult formulas are wrong for under-18s and for pregnancy or breastfeeding. Higher protein is unsafe with some kidney conditions. No automatic estimate for anyone under 18 or pregnant or breastfeeding. They get manual targets only, with a "talk to a professional" note. A one-line health notice appears before estimates; it isn't a blocking agreement.
4 Weight loss, gain and maintenance Nothing bounds the size of the change, so an aggressive deficit could be suggested. Fixed, documented defaults: loss = −15% of energy needs (at most −500 kcal per day), gain = +10%. The estimate never goes below the estimated resting energy (BMR). A manual target below 1,200 kcal or protein above 2.2 g/kg gets a warning but isn't blocked, because it's the user's own number.
5 "Adapt to daily targets taking previously eaten meals into account" If an eaten meal has unknown calories, "remaining calories" is unknown too. Subtracting only the known part makes the meal look as if it should be bigger. Remaining amount = target − actual − other planned meals, only when every meal involved has a known value for that nutrient. Otherwise, fall back to a typical share per meal, and say so on the card.
6 "Suggested portions" The app has two kinds of servings: how many the recipe cooks (the household, default 2) and how much you eat. Changing the cooked servings to hit a target would change the shopping list too. Personalization only suggests your portion, from 0.75 to 1.5 servings in 0.25 steps. The number of servings cooked never changes.
7 "Flexible ranges" Ranges aren't defined. Calories: target ±10% counts as on target. Protein and fiber: at or above the target is on target, and there's no penalty for going over. Score falls off linearly outside the range (§3.4).
8 Keto as a dietary style The app only tracks calories, protein and fiber. It can't check carbohydrates, which is what defines keto. Keto also conflicts with the fiber estimate (14 g per 1,000 kcal). Keto works by ingredient filtering only (no grains, sugar, legumes, potatoes or most fruit). It is always labeled "keto-style — carbohydrates not checked". Keto lowers the fiber target to the user's manual value (no estimate). An optional carbs field is listed under deferred work.
9 Mediterranean as a dietary style Mediterranean is a pattern to lean toward, not a list of excluded foods. A hard filter would hide most recipes. Split styles into hard filters (vegetarian, vegan, keto-style, paleo) and soft preferences (Mediterranean). A soft preference adds a small bonus for legumes, fish, vegetables, whole grains and olive oil. It never hides a recipe.
10 "Compatible combinations" Some combinations are redundant or contradictory. Vegan already includes vegetarian. "Omnivorous" means "no restriction", so it can't be combined with vegetarian. Omnivorous is the "no restriction" default. Choosing vegan also covers vegetarian. Vegan + paleo and vegan + keto are allowed, with a note that very few recipes will match.
11 Allergies, intolerances and dislikes They need different handling. Text matching misses hidden sources: soy sauce contains wheat, pesto contains nuts and dairy, and stock may contain celery. Allergies and intolerances are hard filters. They match against a built-in list of terms for each major allergen, and also match even when the ingredient is marked optional. Dislikes hide a recipe unless the disliked ingredient is optional. Composite ingredients (stock, sauce, pesto, curry paste…) get a "check the label" note.
12 "Do not imply filtering guarantees safety" Labels like "allergen-free" or "compatible" would imply exactly that. The wording is fixed. Own recipes show "No declared allergens found in listed ingredients", never "safe". Ingredients the app can't classify are listed as "not checked: X". External recipes are always "Not verified".
13 Personalization should "supplement, not replace" other priorities This needs to be checked, not just stated. Nutrition keeps its existing 25-point share (§3.4). A test confirms that a recipe with all its ingredients available and nothing eaten recently still beats a better nutritional fit that has missing ingredients.
14 Privacy The current JSON export would include body measurements without saying so. Each field explains why it's needed. Measurements are not required for dietary preferences. There's one "Delete personal data" button that keeps recipes and history. Export asks whether to include personal data and leaves it out by default. No weight history is kept.

2. User-facing behaviour

2.1 Settings → "Nutrition & diet" (replaces "Daily targets" and "Dietary exclusions")

A. Diet & allergies. Always available; no measurements needed.

  • Eating pattern: Omnivorous (default) / Vegetarian / Vegan.
  • Styles (choose any): Paleo, Keto-style, Mediterranean (soft preference).
  • Allergies: chips for common allergens plus free text.
    • EU's 14: gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, peanuts, soy, milk, tree nuts, celery, mustard, sesame, sulphites, lupin, molluscs.
  • Intolerances: free text plus suggestions (lactose, gluten, FODMAP-type items listed as text only).
  • Disliked ingredients: free text.
  • Existing "dietary exclusions" move into "Other exclusions", kept unchanged.

B. Nutrition targets. Off by default, with three states: off, manual, or estimated.

  • Off: nutrition scoring stays neutral. Cards still show any values entered for a recipe.
  • Manual: calories, protein and fiber, each optional. This is today's behaviour.
  • Estimate: a short form → an estimate shown with its range, method and working → "Use these values". After that, every value can be edited, and an edited value is marked as edited.
  • Each field includes a short note on why it's asked, for example "Weight: used to estimate energy and protein needs. Stored only in this browser."
  • There's a permanent "Not medical advice" line plus the limits in §3.6.

2.2 Suggest screen

  • A daily strip with three separate rows, each showing a "partial" note when needed:
    • Actual: from eaten meals.
    • Projected: actual plus remaining planned meals, shown separately and never merged into actual.
    • Room for this meal: or "typical share" when data is incomplete.
  • Each card adds:
    • Suggested portion for you: e.g. "1.25 servings → 610 kcal · 38 g protein · 9 g fiber". When the recipe's nutrition is unknown, it shows "—".
    • Diet line: e.g. "Vegetarian ✓ · No declared allergens found in listed ingredients · Not checked: stock (check label)".
  • The external recipe card says "Diet and allergens: not verified". It never shows ✓.
  • In the "nothing fits" panel, allergy and diet filters are listed as reasons but never offered as one-click changes. This continues the current rule.

2.3 Plan & log

  • The planned portion defaults to the suggested portion, if there is one, and can be edited.
  • Daily nutrition shows Actual / Projected / Target range. It says "Partial: 1 of 3 meals has unknown values" and "No breakfast logged". It never shows a percentage computed from a partial total as if it were complete.

3. Calculations

3.1 Resting energy (BMR): Mifflin–St Jeor (1990)

BMR = 10·kg + 6.25·cm − 5·age + s, where s = +5 (male formula) or −161 (female formula). "Prefer not to say" → compute both and show the range.

This method is widely recommended for estimating BMR in adults, but individual results can be off by about ±10% or more. It is valid for ages 18 and over. It is less reliable for very muscular people and for extremes of body size.

3.2 Total daily energy = BMR × everyday-activity factor + average exercise

Everyday activity (no planned exercise) Factor
Mostly sitting 1.2
Some walking or standing 1.375
On my feet most of the day / physical job 1.55

Exercise is entered as sessions per week × minutes × type, with types light / moderate / vigorous at MET 3.5 / 5 / 8. Exercise kcal per day = Σ (MET − 1) × kg × hours ÷ 7, using MET values from the Compendium of Physical Activities. The −1 avoids counting resting energy twice.

Assumption, documented in the app: the factor table is the usual one with the exercise part left out. Splitting it this way is an approximation, not a validated model.

3.3 Targets (all labeled estimate)

Calories:

  • Maintain: total daily energy (TDEE).
  • Lose: TDEE − min(15% of TDEE, 500).
  • Gain: TDEE + 10% of TDEE.
  • The result is never below BMR, and is rounded to 50 kcal.

Protein: grams per kg of body weight.

Goal Protein
Default 0.8 g/kg (the RDA)
Weight loss 1.2 g/kg
Muscle building (any weight goal) 1.6 g/kg (Morton et al. 2018)

The range shown runs from the target up to 2.2 g/kg.

Fiber: 14 g per 1,000 kcal (Institute of Medicine adequate intake). Hidden if keto-style is on.

3.4 Scoring changes (keeps the 50 / 25 / 25 weights)

  • Nutrition fit for each nutrient, compared against the room for this meal at the suggested portion:
    • Calories: 1 inside ±10%, falling linearly to 0 at ±50%.
    • Protein and fiber: 1 at or above the target, falling linearly to 0 at 0.
  • The nutrition score is the mean over nutrients that have both a target and a value. If none qualify, it's a neutral ½. This is the same as now.
  • Suggested portion = the 0.25 step in [0.75, 1.5] with the best calorie fit. Changed during build: there's no portion suggestion without a calorie target and known recipe calories. Suggesting more of a low-protein dish to reach a protein target produced 1,050 kcal portions in testing.
  • Mediterranean bonus: up to +3 points, from the share of main ingredients in the preferred groups. It's shown separately in the card's breakdown.
  • Dislikes and diet styles are filters. They don't change the score.

3.5 When data is incomplete

  • Each nutrient is handled on its own. A meal with an unknown value is never counted as 0.
  • If any eaten or planned meal for the day has an unknown value for a nutrient, that nutrient's room is the typical share: target ÷ 3 for lunch and dinner, target ÷ 4 for breakfast. The card says "based on typical share — today's log is incomplete".

3.6 Limits shown in the app

  • Estimates come from population averages, not measurements, and may be off by several hundred kcal.
  • Not for under-18s, pregnancy or breastfeeding, or medical conditions.
  • Not medical or dietetic advice.
  • Allergy filtering checks only listed ingredient names. Always read labels.

4. Data model (schema v2, migrated automatically from v1)

settings.diet = {
  pattern: 'omnivore' | 'vegetarian' | 'vegan',
  styles: ['paleo', 'keto', 'mediterranean'],
  allergies: [], intolerances: [], dislikes: [],
  otherExclusions: [/* old settings.exclusions */],
}
settings.nutrition = {
  mode: 'off' | 'manual' | 'estimate',
  targets: { calories, protein, fiber },        // what scoring uses
  edited: { calories: bool, protein: bool, fiber: bool },
  estimate: { method: 'mifflin-st-jeor-1990', computedAt, values, range },
}
profile = { weightKg, heightCm, age, formulaSex: 'male'|'female'|'unspecified',
            everydayActivity, exercise: [{ perWeek, minutes, intensity }],
            weightGoal: 'maintain'|'lose'|'gain', muscleBuilding: bool,
            pregnantOrBreastfeeding: bool }      // deleted by "Delete personal data"
plan[].portion  // the user's own portion, separate from the servings cooked

Upgrading: if v1 had targets, mode becomes 'manual'. Old exclusions become otherExclusions.

New module src/logic/diet.js holds the ingredient classification table: term → groups such as meat, poultry, fish, shellfish, dairy, egg, gluten-grain, legume, peanut, tree-nut, soy, sesame, sugar, starchy, composite. Each allergen and style is a rule over those groups.

5. Build plan

  1. diet.js: the classification table, rules, and dietCheck(recipe) → { pass, reasons, unchecked, composite }, plus tests (soy sauce → gluten, pesto → nut and dairy, vegan ⊃ vegetarian, unclassified ingredients reported).
  2. targets.js: Mifflin, the activity and exercise calculation, goals, guards (under-18 / pregnancy → no estimate), and ranges, tested against worked examples.
  3. recommend.js: the room for this meal (with fallback), range-based fit, the suggested portion, the Mediterranean bonus, and the diet filter, plus the "personalization doesn't override other priorities" test.
  4. Store: schema v2 migration, and export that leaves out personal data by default, plus tests.
  5. UI: the Nutrition & diet settings (manual, estimate, review, edit, delete), the daily strip, card lines, the plan portion, and partial-data notes.
  6. Browser check: personalization off → suggestions match today's; turn it on → estimate → edit → plan with a portion → eat → projected vs actual. Then the allergy hard filter, external "Not verified", deleting personal data, and reloading.
  7. README: method, sources, limits, and what's deferred.

6. Deferred

  • Carbohydrate and fat tracking (needed for a checked keto)
  • Separate targets for training days and rest days
  • Tracking body weight over time
  • Imperial units input (MVP uses kg and cm; easy to add)
  • A nutrition database for calculating recipe values
  • User-confirmed "free-from" labels for each recipe
